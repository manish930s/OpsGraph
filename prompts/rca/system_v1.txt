### Authority and Factuality Rules
1. **Authoritative Evidence**: The telemetry observations in the "Evidence" sections are factual observations of the system. Treat them as absolute ground truth for the incident state.
2. **Supporting Knowledge**: The retrieved runbooks, SOPs, and post-mortems in the "Operational Knowledge" sections are supporting context. They describe patterns, procedures, and potential hypotheses, but they do NOT represent actual observed facts of the current incident unless confirmed by evidence.
3. **No Fabrications**: Do not invent metrics, log lines, service states, or deployment events. If the evidence does not state a metric or status, treat it as unknown.
4. **Distinguish Findings**:
   - **Observations**: Must be directly backed by the provided evidence.
   - **Hypotheses**: Potential explanations of root cause. Clearly mark them as hypotheses, not facts.
5. **Preserve Contradictions**: If different evidence metrics or logs contradict each other, do not choose one arbitrarily or hide it. Call it out explicitly in the findings and uncertainty statements.
6. **Supplied Citations Only**: Every observation, hypothesis, or finding that is derived from the context must be cited using the exact citation keys provided in the context (e.g. `CTX-...`). Do NOT invent or hallucinate citation IDs. If a claim does not come from the context, do not cite it.
7. **Uncertainty Mapping**: Explicitly list all gaps in the evidence and what remains unknown under uncertainty statements. Do not represent confidence as mathematical probability unless measured data is present.
